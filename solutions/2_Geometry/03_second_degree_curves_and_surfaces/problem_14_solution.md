# Problem 14: Cone and Cylinder

## Problem Statement

Compare
$$x^2+y^2=z^2$$
and
$$x^2+y^2=4.$$
For each surface, describe the cross-sections $z=c$, $x=0$, and $y=0$, make a sketch, and explain why the radius of the cross-section depends on $z$ in the first case but is constant in the second.

## Theoretical Background and Concepts

Two fundamental rotationally symmetric second-degree surfaces in $\mathbb{R}^3$ are the **circular cone** and the **circular cylinder**:

1. **Right Circular Cone (Double Cone):**
   $$\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{a^2} = \frac{z^2}{c_0^2} \quad \text{(in simplest form } x^2 + y^2 = z^2\text{)}.$$
   - **Algebraic structure:** All three variables $x, y, z$ appear in the equation as second-degree terms, with $x^2 + y^2$ on one side and $z^2$ on the other.
   - **Geometry:** Generated by rotating a line through the origin ($x = z$ in the $xz$-plane) around the $Oz$-axis. It consists of two nappes (upper for $z \ge 0$ and lower for $z \le 0$) meeting at a sharp **apex (vertex)** at the origin $(0,0,0)$.

2. **Right Circular Cylinder:**
   $$x^2 + y^2 = R^2 \quad (R > 0).$$
   - **Algebraic structure:** The variable $z$ is **completely absent** from the equation (its coefficient is $0 \cdot z = 0$), while $x$ and $y$ satisfy the equation of a circle of radius $R$.
   - **Geometry:** Whenever a coordinate variable is missing from the equation of a surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$, that coordinate is **unrestricted** ($z \in (-\infty, \infty)$). Every point $(x, y)$ on the base circle $x^2 + y^2 = R^2$ in the $xy$-plane can be lifted or lowered to any height $z$, sweeping out vertical lines (**rulings** or **generators**) parallel to the $Oz$-axis.

## Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

---

### Surface 1: $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$ (Right Circular Double Cone)

#### 1. Horizontal Cross-Sections $z = c$:
Substitute $z = c$ into $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$:
$$x^2 + y^2 = c^2.$$
- **For $c = 0$:** $x^2 + y^2 = 0 \implies (x, y) = (0, 0)$. The cross-section is a **single point**, the apex $(0, 0, 0)$.
- **For $c \neq 0$:** $x^2 + y^2 = |c|^2$. The cross-section is a **circle** centered at $(0, 0, c)$ with radius:
  $$R(c) = \sqrt{c^2} = |c|.$$
  As $|c|$ increases, the radius $R(c) = |c|$ grows linearly with distance from the $xy$-plane.

#### 2. Vertical Cross-Section $x = 0$ (in the $yz$-plane):
Substitute $x = 0$ into $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$:
$$y^2 = z^2 \iff z^2 - y^2 = 0 \iff (z - y)(z + y) = 0 \iff z = y \quad \text{or} \quad z = -y.$$
- **Description:** A **pair of intersecting straight lines** $z = y$ and $z = -y$ in the $yz$-plane, crossing at the origin $(0,0,0)$ at $45^\circ$ angles to the axes.

#### 3. Vertical Cross-Section $y = 0$ (in the $xz$-plane):
Substitute $y = 0$ into $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$:
$$x^2 = z^2 \iff z^2 - x^2 = 0 \iff (z - x)(z + x) = 0 \iff z = x \quad \text{or} \quad z = -x.$$
- **Description:** A **pair of intersecting straight lines** $z = x$ and $z = -x$ in the $xz$-plane, crossing at the origin $(0,0,0)$.

---

### Surface 2: $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ (Right Circular Cylinder)

#### 1. Horizontal Cross-Sections $z = c$:
Substitute $z = c$ into $x^2 + y^2 = 4$. Since $z$ does not appear in the equation, the equation in the plane $z = c$ remains:
$$x^2 + y^2 = 4 = 2^2.$$
- **For every $c \in \mathbb{R}$:** The cross-section is a **circle** centered at $(0, 0, c)$ with a **constant radius**:
  $$R(c) = \sqrt{4} = 2.$$

#### 2. Vertical Cross-Section $x = 0$ (in the $yz$-plane):
Substitute $x = 0$ into $x^2 + y^2 = 4$:
$$0^2 + y^2 = 4 \implies y^2 = 4 \implies y = 2 \quad \text{or} \quad y = -2 \quad (\text{with } z \in \mathbb{R} \text{ arbitrary}).$$
- **Description:** A **pair of parallel vertical lines** in the $yz$-plane given by $(0, 2, z)$ and $(0, -2, z)$, each parallel to the $Oz$-axis at distance $2$ from it.

#### 3. Vertical Cross-Section $y = 0$ (in the $xz$-plane):
Substitute $y = 0$ into $x^2 + y^2 = 4$:
$$x^2 + 0^2 = 4 \implies x^2 = 4 \implies x = 2 \quad \text{or} \quad x = -2 \quad (\text{with } z \in \mathbb{R} \text{ arbitrary}).$$
- **Description:** A **pair of parallel vertical lines** in the $xz$-plane given by $(2, 0, z)$ and $(-2, 0, z)$, each parallel to the $Oz$-axis at distance $2$ from it.

---

### Explanation: Why the Cross-Section Radius Depends on $z$ for the Cone but Is Constant for the Cylinder

1. **Algebraic Explanation:**
   - In the **cone equation** $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$, the right-hand side is $z^2$, which explicitly contains the height coordinate $z$. In cylindrical coordinates where $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ is the horizontal distance from the $Oz$-axis, the equation is $r^2 = z^2 \implies r = |z|$. Thus, the horizontal distance $r$ from the $z$-axis is directly tied to the vertical height $z$: changing $z$ forces $r$ to change proportionally.
   - In the **cylinder equation** $x^2 + y^2 = 4$, the variable $z$ does **not appear at all** (equivalently, $x^2 + y^2 + 0 \cdot z = 4$). In cylindrical coordinates, the equation is simply $r^2 = 4 \implies r = 2$. Because this constraint places no condition whatsoever on $z$, a point $(x, y, z)$ lies on the surface if and only if its horizontal projection $(x, y)$ lies on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$, regardless of the value of $z$.

2. **Geometric Explanation (Translation vs. Scaling):**
   - The **cylinder** is translationally invariant along the $Oz$-axis: shifting any cross-section vertically by $\Delta z$ produces the exact same circle of radius $2$. Its vertical cross-sections ($x = \pm 2$ or $y = \pm 2$) are **parallel lines** that maintain a fixed distance $2$ from the $Oz$-axis.
   - The **cone** is scale-invariant (homothetic) from the origin: its vertical cross-sections ($z = \pm x$ or $z = \pm y$) are **lines through the origin that diverge** as $|z|$ increases, so their distance $|z|$ from the $Oz$-axis grows linearly with height.

### Summary Comparison Table

| Feature | Cone: $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$ | Cylinder: $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Presence of $z$ in equation** | Present on RHS ($z^2$) | Absent ($0 \cdot z$) |
| **Cross-section $z = c$** | Circle of radius $R(c) = |c|$ (point at $c=0$) | Circle of constant radius $R(c) = 2$ for all $c$ |
| **Cross-section $x = 0$** | Two intersecting lines $z = \pm y$ at $(0,0,0)$ | Two parallel vertical lines $y = \pm 2$ |
| **Cross-section $y = 0$** | Two intersecting lines $z = \pm x$ at $(0,0,0)$ | Two parallel vertical lines $x = \pm 2$ |

## Verification and Consistency Checks

1. **Cone ($x^2 + y^2 = z^2$) at Different Heights $z = c$:**
   - At $z = 0$: $(0, 0, 0) \implies 0^2 + 0^2 = 0^2$ ($R(0) = 0$). $\checkmark$
   - At $z = 2$: point $(2, 0, 2) \implies 2^2 + 0^2 = 4 = 2^2$ ($R(2) = 2$). $\checkmark$
   - At $z = -5$: point $(3, 4, -5) \implies 3^2 + 4^2 = 9 + 16 = 25 = (-5)^2$ ($R(-5) = 5$). $\checkmark$

2. **Cylinder ($x^2 + y^2 = 4$) at Different Heights $z = c$:**
   - At $z = 0$: point $(2, 0, 0) \implies 2^2 + 0^2 = 4$ ($R(0) = 2$). $\checkmark$
   - At $z = 2$: point $(2, 0, 2) \implies 2^2 + 0^2 = 4$ ($R(2) = 2$). $\checkmark$
   - At $z = -100$: point $(0, -2, -100) \implies 0^2 + (-2)^2 = 4$ ($R(-100) = 2$). $\checkmark$
   *(Notice that at $z = \pm 2$, both surfaces have a circle of radius $2$, which means the cone $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$ and cylinder $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ intersect along the two circles $z = 2$ and $z = -2$ of radius $2$.)*
